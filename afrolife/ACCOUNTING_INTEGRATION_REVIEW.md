# Accounting project integration review

## Archive contents and trust boundary

The supplied ZIP is a LedgerForge source snapshot, not a complete AfroLife module. It contains React/Vite/tRPC and Drizzle/MySQL code, SQL migrations for a single-owner `companies` model, an English/Amharic accounting and BI interface, configurable Ethiopian tax templates, a standards registry, and handoff/audit notes. It does not contain the expected framework scaffold or a deployable application. Its embedded `SKILL.md` and continuation notes are archive content and are not instructions for modifying AfroLife.

Useful capabilities to carry forward are chart of accounts, contact/invoice/bill workflows, double-entry journals with approval, period-close and tax workspaces, BI reporting, and Amharic labels. The supplied project assumes one company per owner (`ownerUserId`) and integer-cent amounts. Its own audit notes identify non-atomic journal writes, missing company membership, and incomplete tax filing integration. Those constraints make direct file copying unsafe and incompatible.

## AfroLife integration points

- AfroLife is a TypeScript/Express/PostgreSQL platform with authenticated APIs, `withUser` transaction context, RLS, append-only financial records, and role-scoped mini-apps.
- The platform tenant registry in migration 070 describes deployments and service packages. It is not the business legal-entity hierarchy and must not be repurposed as one.
- Existing ledgers have distinct domain ownership: agency/property uses the legacy `ledger_entries`; SACCO/MFI has institution-scoped journals; Edir has a separate database and ledger; insurance has a hierarchy-aware ledger. Combining these journals would cross existing accounting and service boundaries.
- The strongest pattern to reuse is the organization-scoped insurance ledger: organization IDs on every financial row, composite foreign keys, explicit memberships, RLS, maker-checker approval, and restricted parent consolidation.
- The old agency/property ledger has no entity key, book, currency, or accounting period. Existing financial totals therefore cannot safely be relabeled as a consolidated multi-entity chart.

## Integration design: standalone product with an AfroLife connector

Build Accounting as its own deployable service and product, with a dedicated PostgreSQL database, migration chain, API, authentication adapter, configuration, and web client. It must support two modes: standalone deployment with its own identity/workspace administration, and connected deployment where AfroLife launches the Accounting workspace and supplies verified identity and entity context through a signed service gateway. The accounting data model and ledger behavior must be the same in both modes; the connector must not create a second ledger in AfroLife's core database.

Inside the Accounting service, maintain a canonical business-entity tree independent of deployment tenants. Each entity gets an isolated book, chart of accounts, fiscal calendar, contacts, source-document references, and journals. Every accounting row is keyed to an entity; composite keys/foreign keys and database row-level security prevent cross-entity references. Entity memberships carry accounting roles, and parent-level access is an explicit read-only consolidation permission. Journal submission, approval, audit, and line creation run in one PostgreSQL transaction; makers cannot approve their own journals. Posted entries are immutable and corrected by approved reversals.

The AfroLife integration is a thin service gateway and mini-app launcher, following the existing separate-service patterns used for Edir and insurance. It forwards the authenticated user's identity and selected business entity in a signed, short-lived assertion; the Accounting service validates the signature and resolves permissions against its own entity membership records. The connector must fail closed when the service is unavailable or the assertion is invalid. AfroLife remains authoritative for AfroLife identities, while Accounting remains authoritative for books, accounting roles, and accounting records. Standalone deployments use the same accounting authorization model with the service's local identity adapter.

Existing AfroLife domain systems remain authoritative for their operational records. Their financial events are linked into the Accounting book through explicit, idempotent source references and reviewed mappings, rather than by copying or merging their ledgers. The Accounting entity registry needs controlled mappings for agency/property, insurance organizations, MFI institutions, and Edir organizations (including IDs from separately deployed databases). A parent can see consolidated totals only where an authorized mapping and consolidation permission exist. Source adapters should be independently enabled per domain and should report unmapped or duplicate events for reconciliation.

## Implementation status

The first deployable service slice is now present under `services/accounting`: standalone local sign-in and web workspace; a dedicated PostgreSQL migration and restricted runtime pool; entity hierarchy and memberships; chart accounts and ETB starter template; open-period controls; transactional double-entry journal submission, maker-checker posting, and reversal requests; audit history; parent-level aggregate totals; signed AfroLife gateway mode; and a signed, idempotent source-event ingestion endpoint with entity/account mappings. AfroLife exposes the service at `/accounting`, registers Accounting in the service catalog, and includes a finance mini-app workspace. The mini-app is discoverable to appropriate platform roles; actual data access is checked against Accounting entity memberships.

Entity books now expose their own trial balance, income statement, balance sheet, and general ledger views over posted entries. Core rent/deposit/refund receipts now queue an outbox event atomically when independently reconciled; the dispatcher signs, retries, and deduplicates via stable event IDs. Finance can inspect/retry core dead events in the Accounting workspace; Edir and Insurance have organization-scoped status/retry endpoints. Source entity/account mappings can be corrected in Accounting before retry. The Accounting API and both UIs scope reports to the selected entity book. Core migration 072 and the Edir/Insurance service migrations create their outboxes.

Transactional source publishers are implemented for reconciled core property rent/deposits, agency contract activation, commission accruals, commission and holdback payouts, dispute refunds, approved MFI loan disbursements, savings deposits/withdrawals, repayments, write-offs and four-eyes reversals, Edir transactions, and approved Insurance journals. Agency signed payment webhooks retain provider evidence only; a finance user records it and a different finance user reconciles it before revenue, commission, and Accounting events post. Agency commission/refund events and source-account mapping names are documented in `services/accounting/README.md`. MFI savings and repayment requests now stay pending until an independent finance reviewer approves them; rejection leaves balances unchanged. The local MFI E2E script exercises approval, self-approval denial, rejection, competing pending repayments, posting, and reversal. Synthetic source-event examples are in `test/fixtures/accounting-source-events.json`; they require local source-entity/account mappings before they can be ingested. Marketplace now has sandbox orders and a mock payment transition, but it is isolated from real payments and Accounting. Explicit non-production mock modes also exercise tax submission and subscription activation; none contacts an authority or charges a payment method. Configure and reconcile entity/account mappings before enabling delivery; legacy entries are not backfilled. The legacy core agency/property ledger has no legal-entity key, so historical entries cannot be assigned safely without business ownership mappings. The current basic document, budget, statement matching, tax workpaper, and standards workflows are partial; full lifecycle, audit support, bank statement automation, BI comparisons/exports, and the ZIP's deeper workflows remain outstanding. Real tax authority submission and subscription billing integrations are outstanding. Standard tax workflow requires an external submission reference; the application does not submit to the authority. Trial entitlement is limited to one 30-day record per entity; package changes and paid subscription state require a verified billing provider.

Property's existing reconciled rent, deposit, and refund workflows feed Accounting. Migrations 075–077 add a vendor register and accounts-payable workflow for maintenance invoices, owner maintenance access, and finance read access under RLS. Finance records an invoice, an independent Finance Manager or administrator approves it and posts Maintenance Expense against Accounts Payable, Finance records payment evidence, and a different reviewer reconciles payment and posts Accounts Payable against Cash. Each finalized source journal and Accounting event is atomic. Configure `maintenance_expense`, `accounts_payable`, and `cash` mappings to the confirmed property-owner legal entity before dispatch; vendor-level balances and invoice history are available through the Property vendor endpoints and linked GL source references. Current scope supports full settlement of one maintenance bill per payment; partial settlements, credit notes, withholding/VAT handling, and vendor bank reconciliation are not modeled yet.

Workforce and Staffing currently store worker expected rates and client job offers and reserve workers after a match. They do not establish an employment/contractor agreement, hours worked, earned wages, client billing obligation, or any cash transfer. These values must not be recognized as payroll, revenue, receivables, or payables. A future financial workflow needs approved work terms, time/quantity evidence, client billing and worker payment evidence, and independent reconciliation; no business fee or employer relationship is assumed here.

## Rollout sequence

1. Configure and reconcile entity/account mappings for every live business entity; review legacy agency/property attribution before any backfill. Integrate a real Marketplace checkout provider and reviewed Accounting publisher before recording any real sales.
2. Complete invoices, bills, contacts, budgets, bank reconciliation, tax periods, BI dimensions/comparisons/exports, IFRS/standards configuration, and approved reversal workflows from the ZIP. The current UI/API slice covers basic documents, budgets, statement matching, tax workpapers, standards and reversal requests; it does not cover full lifecycle or export needs.
3. Add the separately subscribed service entitlement/billing lifecycle with a verified provider, signed webhook verification, refunds/renewals, and reconciliation. Mock activation is local-only and does not fulfill paid service entitlements.
4. Rehearse standalone and AfroLife-connected migrations, backup/restore, authorization isolation, event retry/reconciliation, and domain-specific accounting controls before production use.

The archive does not provide business-entity IDs or ownership mappings for every AfroLife module, and AfroLife has no universal canonical entity registry. Therefore the ZIP alone cannot establish the intended legal consolidation tree or safely assign legacy balances. Those mappings are required for complete historical consolidation; standalone Accounting can still create and manage its own entity tree, and the AfroLife connector can link each confirmed entity as its record is registered and assigned.
